- Schlei / Schleimünde (2026-10-18, user report, fix pending rebuild v50): (1) passage-vertex links were allowed to cross up to 150 m of land anywhere ("a mole"); the Lotseninsel spit at Schleimünde is narrower, so the fairway chain inside the Schlei was linked straight across the island to the water node east of it and routes cut through the island instead of using the mole gap. Scattered land is now limited to 50 m (two samples); the leading-land rule (≤ 1.5 km from the start point, Siel harbours) is unchanged. (2) The ×3 channel band existed only around buoy chains ("Fahrwasser"); the middle Schlei (Lindaunis–Arnis) has only an OSM fairway line ("Wasserstraße (n OSM-Wege)"), so the shortest path hugged the inner bank at the same price as the fairway. The band now also surrounds the OSM waterway lines (builder and smoother use the same set). The depth overlay was not the cause: the whole Schlei is one 1.83–3.66 m step, the route was identical without a draft.

- **Schlei was land (2026-10-19, f7b38a5).** v50 (50 m link rule, ×3 band around OSM waterway lines) changed nothing in the Schlei: `routing_land` covered the whole Schlei (no water node between Schleswig and Schleimünde, every buoy "on land"). OSM maps the Schlei as `natural=water` + `water=fjord`, not as coastline, and the water import only took `lake|reservoir|lagoon`. So routes could only follow the OSM river line (the bank hugging) and the harbour-basin links through the Lotseninsel were allowed as "leading land". Fix: `water=fjord` in the Overpass water query, water re-imported over the whole region (pipeline16), rebuild v51. v50 numbers for the record: 4240/4556 harbours reachable (v49: 4271, the 50 m rule cost 31 links across land), Utö→Dalarö 15.9 nm (straight 15.6, so the earlier 12 nm target was wrong), Langeland→Svendborg +8 % vs NavShip, Zeewolde→Harlingen +13 %. e2e: `GET /trips/{id}/map` returned 500 (`ST_Y(geography)`), fixed in Trips.cs.

- **Walking distances to the harbour services (2026-10-20).** Table `walk_edge` (migration `WalkNetwork`, raw SQL: id, source/target = OSM node ids, length_m, geom 4326, GiST) built by `tools/build-walknet.sh [poly] [pbf]`: osmium (WSL) tags-filter `highway=` footway/path/pedestrian/steps/living_street/residential/service/unclassified/road/track/cycleway/bridleway/crossing/corridor/platform/tertiary…primary(+links)/trunk(+links) → extract with the 5 km harbour polygon (`GET /api/admin/map/harbour-areas.poly?radiusKm=5`, 2,740 rings) → `add-locations-to-ways` OPL → `tools/walknet.cs` (file-based .NET app: splits ways at nodes shared by ≥ 2 ways, drops foot=no/use_sidepath, access=no without foot permission, trunk without foot permission or sidewalk; access=private stays, marinas are private) → COPY. Source `europe-latest.osm.pbf` because the harbours are Europe-wide (`wide.osm.pbf` covers only 7,262 of 13,910): 23.5 M ways, 22.9 M kept, 123 M nodes, **45.6 M edges, 9.2 GB** (table + index); 34 min (tags-filter 22 min, extract 5 min, convert + COPY + index 7 min). `Sms.Domain.WalkNetwork`: CSR adjacency, single-source Dijkstra from several start snaps (offset counts), `DistanceTo`/`PathTo` over destination snaps (same-edge case straight along it), legs as edge fractions. `Sms.Api.Walking`: snap = 8 KNN edges per point, real metres (`ST_DistanceSphere`), start ≤ 1 km, place ≤ 500 m; edges loaded in the box the limit reaches. Semantics: walk known → it decides nearest and range; ways near the place but none reached within the limit → dropped (other side of the fjord); no way near the place or the harbour → straight distance stands (`walkM` null). `compute-services` stores `walkM` next to `distanceM` (nearest by walk ≤ 1.5 km among the 25 straight-nearest per service, 8 harbours in parallel); harness over all 13,910 harbours (1.16 M harbour–POI pairs, all kinds) 38.5 s, 9 harbours without network, walk/straight median 1.25 (p90 1.57). `GET /api/pois/near` returns `walkM`, sorts/filters by it (3/5 km rule on the walk), 5 km search cached 5 min per point (Hamburg 120 ms); `GET /api/pois/walk?lat&lng&poiId` → `{walkM, coordinates}` (cached 30 min, 404 = no path). Web: "350 m zu Fuß" in chips, list and popup (`formatServiceDistance(…, onFoot)`), hover draws the walking path solid (`setServicePath`), straight dashed until it arrives or when there is none. Piers (man_made=pier) are not in the network yet: the straight snap offset covers the way from the berth to the first path.

- **Piers in routing (2026-10-19, user report "routing ignores piers").** Piers, jetties, floating pontoons, breakwaters and groynes (`man_made=pier|breakwater|groyne` ways; quays are coastline) are stored as `RoutingCorrections` of the new kind `Pier` (source Derived, line or polygon; kind is a string column, so no migration). Import: `POST /api/admin/routing/import-piers?bbox=south,west,north,east` (Overpass, replaces the derived piers in the bbox, clears `RouteCaches`); the PBF import picks them up with the waterways. Routing: after the string pulling, `PierAvoidance.Apply` (Sms.Domain) reroutes every leg that crosses a pier buffered by 5 m (legs tested against 3 m): a local visibility graph over the convex corners of the merged buffers in a box around the crossing (±300 m along the leg), A*, land/low bridges/shallow areas/channel zone of the smoother still blocking. A route end on a pier gets the nearest water point next to it (seen from the approaching side), the last step onto the pier is allowed; no detour found = leg kept, never no route. Piers are loaded once per process into an STRtree (reset by the import); timing key `smooth-piers` (synthetic marina with 120 finger piers: ~135 ms, legs without a pier crossing: ~0). Tests: `PierAvoidanceTests` (head of a jetty, land blocking both ends, harbour on a jetty, harbour between two jetties). Western Baltic bbox has ~9,500 pier ways.
- **Xweather particles + forecast time slider (2026-10-19, user report "no wind/wave particles").** Root cause: MapsGL's `MaplibreMapController` initialises at once only if `map.isStyleLoaded()`, otherwise it waits for the map's `load` event. Our map fired `load` long before (createMap awaits it), but `isStyleLoaded()` is false while any tiles/sources are still loading (panning, freshly added overlays), so the controller often waited forever for a second `load`: no error, no fallback, no weather layer. `WeatherOverlays.controller()` now reports the style as loaded during construction (it is), with a 20 s timeout that falls back to the raster tiles. When it did load, particles were also near-invisible: coloured by MapsGL's speed scale, the same as the wind-speed field beneath. Wind particles are now white, wave particles dark blue, size 2. Plan/keys allow particles (`wind-particles`, `wave-particles` load and render; verified in Chromium). New: while a GL weather overlay is on, a compact range slider at the bottom of the map (Jetzt, +1…+72 h, label = local weekday/time) sets the MapsGL timeline (range now−1 h…now+73 h, `goToDate`) for all active layers; forecast tiles load for the chosen hour. The raster fallback stays current-only (`Weather.cs` requests `current.png`), so the slider is hidden in raster mode. Vanilla map controls get ngx-translate through `appI18n` in `core/site.ts` (`weather.time.*` keys).

- **In-memory A\*, edge snapping, passage-aware smoothing (2026-10-19, user report "Grömitz → IJsselmeer takes a minute").** Measured on v55 (old = live API before, new = `tools/RouteBench` against the dev DB, warm): the minute was not pgr_aStar (0.8 s) but the string pull (53 s: every point tested against every later point, the Kiel Canal's 2 780 vertices included) plus an 8 s snap (sight test of all nodes in a 40 km box against the 146 000-point Dutch land polygon). Now: `SeaGraph` (Sms.Domain) holds a graph version in memory – node coordinates by id, edges as flat arrays with CSR adjacency, float cost/depth/clearance, only the interior points of multi-point edges (~112 MB for 524 k nodes / 3.03 M edges, loaded lazily per version in ~2 s, old versions dropped, logged); A\* with a haversine heuristic scaled by the minimum cost per metre over all edges (0.99), same filters (air draft, draft + margin with the HarbourAccessM/end-node exemption, ×3 shallow) → 5–25 ms. Snap order for map clicks: a harbour with `routing_harbour_access` within 150 m → its access node; else the node snap (sight test now on the 64 nearest nodes, in memory against the clipped land) unless a passage edge ≥ 1 km is closer – then the edge is split virtually at the projection (Kiel Canal click follows the canal instead of 4 km across fields). Smoothing: free legs densified every 1 km, then a shortest-chain DP over the path's points (greedy farthest-visible got stuck behind the Fehmarnsund bridge); passage segments stay fixed (no cut inside, no skipping a stretch, join late / leave early ≤ `Routing:PassageTrimM` 3 km) unless charted deep enough all along; the channel zone blocks only where the leg is not in a depth area ≥ draft + margin (it blocked every corner next to a buoy chain: the Fehmarnsund detour east to 11.165 E and the wide berth off Laboe). ## Design: incremental graph builds and OSM diff updates (planned)

Every derived routing input is already per bbox chunk (waterway/no-go chunks, fairway chunks, channel chunks, lake chunks) and the land union is computed per 2° chunk; only the build forgets it between runs. Two layers:

1. **Graph build per chunk.** Partition the region into the 2° chunks plus a margin for legs that cross a border. Per chunk, hash the inputs: land tile ids and mtimes, active corrections intersecting the chunk (id + version stamp), depth areas, the routing options. Store per chunk the obstacle polygons, nodes, visibility legs and contracted passages together with the hash; a rebuild recomputes only chunks whose hash changed and redoes the cheap global steps (component merge, harbour attachment, activation). The margin makes a changed chunk refresh the legs that reach into its neighbours. A Europe-scale build becomes a one-time cost, a correction in the Kiel Bight recomputes one chunk in minutes.
2. **OSM updates from replication diffs.** Keep the import's position (`osmosis_replication_timestamp`/sequence of the extract), pull minutely or hourly diffs from the Geofabrik or planet feed, apply them to sea marks, harbours, POIs, waterways, bridges, restricted areas and lakes; every applied element marks the chunks it touches as dirty. The derivations (fairways from lateral marks, channels from land, no-go blocks) rerun for dirty chunks only, then layer 1 picks them up. Prerequisite: relation handling in the PBF import (lakes and multipolygon restricted areas come from Overpass today).

Build time today (v47, 34 min): land + lakes 5 min, chunk union 3 min, nodes and visibility legs 5 min, pricing 1 min, passages and their links 8 min, gap links / component merge / harbour attachment 10 min. Before layer 1: cache the land union per chunk, do every nearest-node lookup of the passage phase in memory, and skip the gap links to an end's own line (fb6ba78).
